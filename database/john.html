Did you happen to know?

The name John originates from the Hebrew name Yoḥanan (יוֹחָנָן), meaning "Yahweh is gracious"  or "God has shown grace."  It passed through Greek Ioannes, Latin Iohannes, and Old French forms before developing into the English name John.

Related forms of the name appear in the Old Testament, including Johanan and Jehohanan.

The name became prominent in Christian tradition through John the Baptist and John the Apostle, two important figures in the New Testament.
John the Baptist is known for baptizing Jesus, while John the Apostle is traditionally associated with the Gospel of John.

Their religious significance contributed to the name's spread among Christian communities in Europe.

During the Middle Ages, John became one of the most popular masculine names in England. By the later medieval period, historical name research suggests that approximately one-fifth of English boys received the name. Its popularity continued for centuries, and John was also the most common boys' name in the United States until 1923.

John has numerous international equivalents that developed through different languages and naming traditions. These include Juan in Spanish, Jean in French, Giovanni in Italian, João in Portuguese, and Ivan in Russian and several other Slavic languages.

Other related forms include Ian in Scottish usage and Sean in Irish.

Despite their similar spelling, John and Jonathan have different origins and meanings.
John derives from Hebrew Yoḥanan, associated with divine grace, whereas Jonathan derives from Yehonatan (יְהוֹנָתָן), meaning "Yahweh has given" or "God has given."
So, they are separate names with distinct etymologies.
