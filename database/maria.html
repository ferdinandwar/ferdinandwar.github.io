 Do you happen to know?

The name Maria translates to the original Hebrew name Miriam.

In first-century Israel, the historical Mary of Nazareth was not royalty, but a young Jewish peasant girl living in a tiny, rural farming village.

Recent archaeological excavations in Nazareth have uncovered the remains of simple, first-century mud-brick and limestone homes from her era. These discoveries reveal that she would have spent her days grinding grain, carrying water from the village spring, and using expensive limestone vessels rather than cheap pottery to strictly adhere to Jewish purity laws.

The name also came from the ancient Egyptian root mr or mry, which means 'love' or 'beloved'.

While the name Maria transitioned into Latin and Greek from the Hebrew Miriam, many modern historical scholars believe its oldest roots are actually Egyptian.
